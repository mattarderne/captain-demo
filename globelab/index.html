Globe lab

The smoke lab's solver, wrapped onto a sphere. The same Stam scheme runs inside an equirectangular texture — longitude by latitude — with the metric terms a curved surface adds, then that texture is painted onto a hand-rolled globe mesh. She sails a great circle around it, into the wind she's trimmed for.

The air

Longitude and latitude are not the same size on the ground: a degree of longitude shrinks by cos(latitude) toward the poles. Divergence, the pressure solve and the gradient subtract all carry that factor — see the comment block at the top of globe.js for the derivation. The poles themselves are a true singularity of this grid; they are clamped and damped rather than solved.

The smoke

Seeded along the meridian at longitude zero, all the way from pole to pole, rather than a single windward edge — a sphere has no edge to seed from.

Particles

Dust: one pixel per particle, lagging the flow by the drag time constant. Trails: each particle carries an age, fades in and out over its life, and paints into a fading streak buffer. Both carry the same 1/cos(latitude) correction the solver applies to the dye, wrap at the date line, reflect off the poles, and respawn off a sail or an island rather than sit inside one. The far side of the globe is culled before it reaches the screen.

The rig

Below, fore / aft and athwartships are where each sail is attached, in her own lengths — here that means radians of arc. Each sail pivots about its luff, so angle sheets the leech in and out while the luff stays put.

Islands

Three islands at the Hawaiian chain's real relative spacing and sizes, blown up so they matter to the flow. They are static solids, so the wind accelerates through the channels between them and goes slack in their lees.
