Vortex lab

A circular obstacle in a uniform stream. Watch the wake behind it: past a threshold speed it stops sitting still and starts alternating, peeling off one vortex to each side in turn — the von Kármán vortex street. Reproduces amandaghassaei's VortexShedding demo: same pipeline, same circular obstacle, a sturdier pressure solve underneath.

Reynolds is U·D/ν from the sliders directly, a real number solved for — but this grid's own numerical dissipation adds to it, same as the original demo's does, so treat it as a lower bound on the total damping at work rather than a literal match to a real cylinder's Re. Shedding frequency is a live, noisy estimate off one probe point downstream — give it a few seconds after any change before trusting it. Textbook Strouhal for a real cylinder sits near 0.2; this solver's own numerical diffusion generally pulls it somewhat lower.

Presets
The flow

Viscosity is a real Jacobi diffusion pass, not a fudge — it's what the Reynolds number above is computed from. Vorticity confinement isn't in the original demo at all (it relies on the advection step's own numerical diffusion); it's here to push the wake further into unsteadiness than viscosity alone allows. Leave it at 0 to see the demo as originally built.

The obstacle

Radius and position are fractions of the channel, so they hold their meaning across window sizes. Bigger radius or slower viscosity both raise the effective Reynolds number and shed harder.

The dye

Alternating stripes fed in at the inflow, the same trick the original demo uses — a plain wash of dye shows you speed but not rotation; stripes let you actually count the vortices as they roll up.

The obstacle

What the flow runs into. The cylinder is what sheds the von Kármán street above and is the default; the rig is the same jib-and-main section every other lab sails with, dropped in here as a second solid shape. "Both" masks in whichever cells either one occupies.
