Placement

Depth is the GROUP's own reference point, straight down from the free surface. Each foil's own offset then moves it independently up or down from that shared depth — zero both sides is the original rigidly-paired behaviour. Drag the assembly on the canvas to set group depth and position at once; the offsets and gap travel with it unchanged.

Two-phase mode

Not stable under sustained flow. With the horizontal flow running, the water fraction measurably drains toward zero over roughly 8–15 seconds regardless of gravity — more iterations and a softer density ratio slow this but do not stop it. The interface deforms convincingly for the first few seconds after a reset, long enough to see the mechanism, then the drain takes over. Do not read a ventilation depth out of a long session here; the linear mode's depth sweep is unaffected by any of this and remains the trustworthy one.

Real water:air is about 1000:1 — too stiff for a fixed-iteration Jacobi solve at this grid to hold together, so this ratio is softened, same as graphics fluid codes routinely do. No surface tension either, so no correctly-scaled droplets or spray. Switch modes with the button above the readouts — the sim resets when you do, since the two modes drive the domain edges differently.

Dye

High-speed free surface only: no gravity, so no waves, wave drag, ventilation or cavitation. This shows the trend and the mechanism at flying speed, not take-off or the breakdown right at the surface.

Free flight

A wingfoil with an 85 cm, aspect-ratio-9 front wing. The rider holds the board's attitude with their feet and the height is free: she rises until the surface takes away enough lift to match her weight. Climb too far and the front wing breaches and ventilates. Trim sets the attitude for the current speed. Then change the speed.

Depth sweep

Steps the assembly from deep to just under the surface, holds each depth long enough to settle and average, and records lift. This curve is the actual answer to "what happens at different depths" — not a single reading.

Offset sweep

Group depth held fixed; the relative depth between the two foils swept instead. Negative: stabiliser shallower than the main foil. Positive: deeper. The case a rigidly-paired assembly can never show — the two feel different amounts of surface effect only once they are not at the same depth.
