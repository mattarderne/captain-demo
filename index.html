The wind over the ball — new A stylised water-ball with a savage horizon. The wind blows from a point in space and flows over the sphere: light on the nose, hardest on the limb, a sheltered lee full of back-eddies behind. Each boat blankets the air downwind of her, so you can sail over your rival and kill her wind, or drop over the curve and vanish where she cannot follow you. Shrink the ball until she is gone three lengths away. Smoke lab — workbench A fluid solver on a flat plane, with a boat sitting in it. Wind blows left to right, she blocks it, and the wake sheds off her in a vortex street. Drag to push the air about. Built flat and on its own so the air can actually be seen while it is being tuned. Globe lab — workbench The smoke lab's solver wrapped onto a sphere: the same equirectangular grid an atlas uses, with the metric terms a curved surface adds carried through the divergence, the pressure solve and the gradient by hand. She sails a great circle round it, into a wind that never runs out of room to blow. Star lab — workbench A reimplementation of JoTrdl's Star Fluid Experiment: 262,144 particles riding a single-pass compressible fluid solver, drawn additively as gold points over a cool dye field. Three lobes orbit and feed it on their own; drag to add your own. Vortex lab — workbench A circular obstacle in a uniform stream, reproducing amandaghassaei's VortexShedding demo: past a threshold speed the wake stops sitting still and starts peeling off one eddy to each side in turn, the von Kármán street a bridge deck or a smokestack sheds in real wind. Drag to push the air, or turn the dials and watch the shedding frequency and Strouhal number update live. Foil lab — workbench A hydrofoil near the surface: a main wing forward, an inverted stabiliser aft, both draggable and independently adjustable in depth. The top of the tank is the free surface, held at zero pressure rather than treated as a wall, which is what makes lift actually fall as she comes up shallow instead of rising the way a rigid lid would. Run the depth sweep for the measured curve. A second, switchable mode tries an honest two-phase water/air interface with gravity and variable density — it deforms convincingly for a few seconds and then drains; that limitation is stated plainly on the panel rather than hidden. The flat ocean The original: a Napoleonic-era ship on an FFT sea, commanded entirely by voice. Needs your own OpenAI key.
